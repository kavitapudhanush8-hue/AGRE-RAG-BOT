TITLE: Groundnut (Peanut) Cultivation Guide

SOIL REQUIREMENTS
Groundnut prefers well-drained sandy loam soils with good calcium content, since the developing pods absorb calcium directly from the soil. Ideal pH is 6.0-6.5. Heavy clay soils restrict pod development.

SOWING TIME
Groundnut is typically sown at the start of the rainy season (kharif) or under irrigation in the post-rainy/summer season, depending on the region. Timely sowing after adequate soil moisture is available improves germination.

SEED TREATMENT
Seed treatment with a fungicide and rhizobium culture before sowing improves germination and enhances nitrogen fixation through root nodules.

IRRIGATION
Groundnut is relatively drought-tolerant but critical stages for irrigation are flowering, pegging (when pegs enter the soil), and pod development. Moisture stress during pegging can significantly reduce pod set.

FERTILIZER MANAGEMENT
Being a legume, groundnut fixes atmospheric nitrogen and needs comparatively low nitrogen fertilizer, but responds well to phosphorus and gypsum (calcium and sulphur source) applied at flowering to support pod filling.

COMMON DISEASES
Tikka leaf spot (early and late leaf spot) causes circular brown to black spots on leaves, leading to defoliation if untreated. Collar rot and stem rot appear as wilting with rotting at the collar/stem base, especially in wet conditions. Rust shows as orange-brown pustules on the underside of leaves.

COMMON PESTS
Leaf miner larvae tunnel inside leaves creating pale, irregular mines. Aphids and thrips are sap-sucking pests that can also spread viral diseases. White grubs damage roots and pods underground, sometimes causing significant yield loss.

HARVESTING
Groundnut is ready for harvest when the inside of the pod shell darkens and the kernel fills out, generally 90-120 days after sowing depending on variety.
